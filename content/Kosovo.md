# Kosovo

**Kosovo** is a country in the Balkans, with its capital at Prishtina (also spelled Pristina). It declared independence from Serbia on February 17, 2008, three weeks after [[The KmikeyM Launch]]. In 2026 it became a second home for [[Mike Merrill]]: he spent two months living in Prishtina in the spring, running [[KmikeyM]] and [[The Agent Fleet|his agent fleet]] remotely, and returned in the fall to speak at Software Freedom Kosova, the country's annual open-source conference.

## Why Kosovo

Merrill had friends in Prishtina who had been urging him to come and work from there. The city offered low living costs, plenty of coffee, good internet, a young, English-speaking tech scene, and the timing suited him: he had moved out of his house and was between homes. The plan was the same work in a cheaper place. He would keep running KmikeyM and see what a new backdrop produced.

## Spring 2026: two months in Prishtina

Merrill arrived in early April 2026 and stayed through early June. He ran the shareholder business from Prishtina, including the May 2026 vote to put $5,000 of KmikeyM's shareholder fund into Dead Internet Theory, a series of in-person retro cyber-café events in Los Angeles. He noted in the proposal that he would be in Kosovo through early June and could not attend the event himself (see [[List of Votes]]).

He also found his way into Kosovo's open-source community, which centers on FLOSSK (Free Libre Open Source Software Kosova) and the Prishtina Hackerspace. In May he helped organize **Trash, Please**, a weekend civic hackathon about understanding the trash infrastructure of Prishtina, with parts built around free European satellite imagery (the code is on GitHub: [kmikeym/trash](https://github.com/kmikeym/trash)).

## Software Freedom Kosova 2026

Merrill came back in late September for the 11th Software Freedom Kosova (SFK), FLOSSK's annual conference, held October 1 to 3, 2026 at the Palace of Youth and Sports in Prishtina.

### "The Cathedral and the Bizarre"

On October 2 he gave a talk titled "The Cathedral and the Bizarre," subtitled "I incorporated myself in 2008 and got pulled toward the bazaar." The title plays on Eric S. Raymond's 1997 essay "The Cathedral and the Bazaar," which contrasted software built by a closed group of insiders with software built in the open by many contributors. The connection to open source was an old one: in 2000, Merrill's job title at LinuxFund was "Operations Penguin."

His argument was that he had modeled his life on a publicly traded company, with rules, disclosure and eventually a board, and that eighteen years later KmikeyM works more like an open-source project, with shareholders acting as contributors and votes working like pull requests. The conference program summed it up in his words: "I modeled my life on a publicly traded company and eighteen years later I have something closer to an open source project. I should have recognized the idea was too bizarre to be a cathedral."

He opened with the year's backlash against AI-generated contributions in open source, from curl ending its bug bounty program to Godot and Codeberg restricting AI-written code. The question-and-answer session covered what happens to the stock when Merrill dies, share counts and pricing, dividends, and AI in the Linux kernel. FLOSSK posted the video on November 9, 2026 (34 minutes, including the Q&A): [SFK 11th Edition - K Mike Merrill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZzXMinQ5GpM).

### Capture the Flag

During the conference Merrill played his first Capture the Flag, a hacking contest in which teams break into deliberately vulnerable programs to retrieve hidden strings called flags. He joined a young FLOSSK volunteer's team, Legends, and worked the challenges by directing an AI coding assistant rather than writing exploit code himself. Legends finished fifth of 20 teams with 22 challenges solved, three of them Merrill's. He wrote it up in [My First Capture The Flag](https://news.kmikeym.com/my-first-capture-the-flag/) (October 9, 2026).
