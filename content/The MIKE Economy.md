# The MIKE Economy

[[KmikeyM]] publishes a fundamentals layer: six indicators tracking [[Mike Merrill]]'s life and output, updated monthly, with quarterly reports written against them. They live at **[mike.quarterly.systems](https://mike.quarterly.systems)**, which carries the charts, the full history back to 2023, and the machine-readable data.

This page carries the current readings so they can be read here, and so questions about them can be answered without leaving the wiki. **Every number on this page has already been published on the dashboard.** Nothing is first-reported here.

## The short answer

**As of October 2026: health back near baseline after two long-haul flights, wealth down again on one-time travel, social reach steady under a new method, and productivity unchanged.**

Health fell from 110.1 to 101.9. Fitness and recovery stayed strong; sleep was the drag, with its consistency score at the floor. The wealth index fell 5.8%, again mostly travel, and part of October's spending lands in next month's row because the index counts cash when it leaves. Social Capital moved to a new method this month (SCI 2.0, see below); on the new method it rose slightly. Productivity was unchanged for the third month, and media dropped from 15 points to 12.

If you want the evidence for that sentence, it is the table below, and the charts are at [mike.quarterly.systems](https://mike.quarterly.systems).

## Why a person has economic indicators

A share of KmikeyM has no earnings behind it and no book value to anchor to, so historically the price moved on sentiment (see [[Share Price History]]). The indicators exist to give shareholders something else to reason from: a set of measures, published on a schedule, that can be tracked over time and argued with.

They are modeled loosely on a central bank's data service. Each is an index where **100 is Mike's own baseline**, not a comparison to anyone else. A reading of 111.6 means "better than his normal," not "good compared to other people."

## Current readings

**As of the November 2026 release, reporting October 2026 actuals.**

The dates are worth stating plainly, because the two are always one month apart. The indicators publish on a **release lag**: a release dated November reports October's numbers, the same way most economic data works. When this page says "as of the November release," the life being measured is October's.

## How to read the notes, not just the numbers

Four of the six readings above need their note to be read correctly. PWI's drop is mostly a one-time travel cost, not a change in how money comes in or goes out, and because the index is cash-basis some of October's spending shows up in November's row. SCI changed method this month, so 105.98 should be compared with October's recomputed 105.53, not with the 101.23 published under the old method. PHI's sleep component is at its floor, so the true sleep reading is lower than the score shows. KBER's level still reflects July's one-time file migration, not a person learning faster.

**SCI 2.0.** Each platform is now measured against its own starting point before it is weighted, so growth on a smaller platform shows up in the index instead of being swamped by the largest one. The old calculation continues as SCI Classic on the dashboard, so neither history is lost.

This is the normal condition rather than an unusual month. A measure of one person's life picks up moves, job changes, injuries and software migrations, and none of those are trends. **The published note is where a distortion gets disclosed**, so a reading without its note is only half the observation.

The indicators also do not aggregate into a single score, deliberately. There is no "MIKE number." Health, wealth, productivity, knowledge, social reach and media consumption move independently, and collapsing them would hide exactly the thing a shareholder is trying to see. October is the case for that: health and wealth both down, productivity flat, social reach up slightly.

## Cadence

- **Monthly.** All six indicators update early in the month, once every input has landed.
- **Quarterly.** A written report goes out against the quarter's data, in the register format the dashboard uses.
- **Corrections.** A published reading is not revised after the fact. Where a number was later found to be wrong, the record says so alongside it rather than replacing it, so the history stays honest about what was known at the time.
